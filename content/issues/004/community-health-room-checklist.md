# A Room That Lets People Participate
*Practical community health checklist — working draft, 28 October 2026*

**For:** a mutual-aid room, library meeting, shared kitchen, repair café, community gathering, or volunteer shift. Reuse and adapt to the space. **Not a medical, building-code, or workplace compliance certificate.** No one should have to disclose their diagnosis to participate.

## Before gathering

- [ ] **Participation without attendance:** Offer telephone, online, written, or asynchronous participation. Make staying home when sick a real option without penalty. Check disability access without asking for medical history. Ask, without requiring disclosure, which languages and communication modes are usable; arrange interpretation, translated materials, captions or another format as needed. Do not assume English or infer needs from identity.
- [ ] **Cleaner air:** Check that the *actual building operator* is maintaining ventilation and appropriate filters. Prefer appropriately sized cleaners with independently stated performance (for example, rated HEPA units) when feasible. When commercial devices are unavailable, a well-built DIY box-fan/filter cleaner may help: use [EPA's assembly, newer-fan safety and maintenance guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/air-cleaners-hvac-filters-and-respiratory-viruses). DIY output varies, so do not claim measured protection without measurement. **Do not use ozone generators in occupied spaces.** Consider outdoors only when safe and accessible.
- [ ] **Weather and outdoor air:** Check whether opening windows would bring in wildfire smoke, bad air, dangerous heat or cold. Do not trade respiratory protection for thermal or pollution hazards.
- [ ] **Respiratory protection:** Offer good-quality, well-fitting masks or respirators without making people ask. Support people who choose to mask and avoid compulsory close contact.
- [ ] **Food, water and sanitation:** Ensure drinking water, handwashing, accessible bathrooms, proper food storage, and clearly identified safe meals are available. A HEPA filter does not make food safe.
- [ ] **Pacing and rest:** Provide seats, rest breaks and an exit/re-entry option. Don't make attendance contingent on exertion. For post-exertional malaise, avoid forced graded activity or "push through it" expectations.
- [ ] **Safe escalation:** Know who can call emergency services, where the exits are, how to reach ordinary medical care, and what to do when someone becomes unwell. Do not substitute volunteer diagnosis for care.

## During gathering

- [ ] Confirm the room ventilation and air cleaner are **actually running** (a poster saying "ventilated" isn't evidence).
- [ ] Keep doors, accessible routes and emergency exits unobstructed; provide seating and adequate rest.
- [ ] Let people leave or switch to remote participation without explanation; do not pressure people to remove masks.
- [ ] Keep indoor air, excessive sound, scent, temperature and access barriers in view alongside infection prevention.

## After gathering — improve the actual room

- [ ] Invite one optional, nonidentifying question: "What made this safer or harder to attend?" Let people decline; a small room cannot promise anonymity, and free-text details can reveal identity.
- [ ] Record the date, room, equipment maintenance, supplies, accessibility problems and changes; **do not** record participants' identities, diagnoses, biometrics, test results or inferred health status.
- [ ] Assign a real person responsible for each fix, with a review date, and check completion at the next event.
- [ ] Communicate corrected measures in ordinary language; do not advertise a room as "infection-proof" or a verified number of air changes without a real measurement.

## When money, power or supplies are constrained

- **No new filters or masks.** Adaptation: pool community supplies, reduce crowding, offer remote options, and consider EPA-guided DIY cleaning when a commercial cleaner is unavailable. **Safety boundary:** do not claim untested devices or dirty filters perform as rated.
- **Electrical outage.** Adaptation: if outdoor conditions and accessibility permit, meet outside; otherwise postpone or go asynchronous. **Safety boundary:** an unplugged cleaner does nothing; never run fuel generators indoors or endanger medical-device power.
- **Poor outdoor air or severe weather.** Adaptation: maintain safe building air handling, postpone, or change format. **Safety boundary:** opening windows may be harmful during smoke, pollution, or temperature extremes.
- **Illness circulating.** Adaptation: support staying home without punishment, reduce indoor exposure, and help deliver essentials. **Safety boundary:** no informal medical registry or pressure to reveal test results.
- **Accessibility gaps.** Adaptation: change schedule, location, transport, format, lighting, seating, or activity demands. **Safety boundary:** "Everyone is welcome" is not evidence everyone can attend.

## Reading and verification

- [CDC: Cleaner air](https://www.cdc.gov/respiratory-viruses/prevention/air-quality.html) — ventilation, outdoor meetings, HEPA filtration, and the need to use actual building capacity.
- [CDC: Respirator and mask protection](https://www.cdc.gov/respiratory-viruses/prevention/masks.html) — fit and type matter.
- [CDC: Prevention when sick](https://www.cdc.gov/respiratory-viruses/prevention/precautions-when-sick.html) — staying home and added precautions, including organization policies.
- [EPA: Air cleaners and respiratory viruses](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/air-cleaners-hvac-filters-and-respiratory-viruses) — tested commercial devices are preferable for routine use when feasible, but well-built DIY cleaners may be effective when commercial devices are unavailable; build and operate safely.
- [CDC: Long COVID clinical guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/long-covid/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html) and [ME/CFS post-exertional malaise guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/me-cfs/hcp/clinical-care/treating-the-most-disruptive-symptoms-first-and-preventing-worsening-of-symptoms.html) — limitations, validation, individualized pacing.

**Scope:** A template for an operator's actual room, not a claim the 561 Group has deployed, measured, certified or staffed a health facility. It requires local hazard, accessibility and scientific review before being represented as a tested intervention.
